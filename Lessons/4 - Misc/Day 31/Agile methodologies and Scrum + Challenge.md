# Agile methodologies and Scrum

For the next four weeks, you will develop a full-stack product as a team. The challenge is no longer only to make your own code work. You must decide what to build first, divide and coordinate the work, combine changes safely, respond when the plan is wrong and keep the whole team moving towards one usable product.

A group can divide a specification into separate tasks without becoming a team. If everyone protects only their own part, integration happens late, knowledge stays with one person and blocked work waits for the next meeting. Agile values and the Scrum framework give the team a different way to organise the work.

## Agile is a way of working

[The Manifesto for Agile Software Development](https://agilemanifesto.org/) describes four values and twelve principles for developing software in uncertain conditions. It asks teams to favour:

- direct collaboration between people instead of depending only on processes and tools
- working software instead of treating extensive documentation as progress
- regular cooperation with customers and stakeholders instead of relying only on a fixed agreement
- adapting to new information instead of following a plan after it has stopped making sense

The items on the right still have value. Agile does not mean "no process", "no documentation" or "no plan". It means that the process, documentation and plan must help people deliver useful software and respond to what they learn.^[https://agilemanifesto.org/ Manifesto for Agile Software Development]^[https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html Principles behind the Agile Manifesto]

Agile is therefore not a meeting schedule or a project board. A team does not become Agile merely by holding a daily meeting, moving tickets or calling a period of work a sprint. The team must deliver in small steps, make the work visible, ask for feedback and change its approach when the evidence shows a problem.

## Scrum is a framework
Scrum is one way to put Agile values into practice. The [[Scrum Guide.pdf|Scrum Guide]] describes it as a lightweight framework for finding adaptive solutions to complex problems. Instead of trying to predict the complete project at the beginning, the team works in short cycles, produces a usable result, inspects what happened and adjusts the next cycle.^[https://scrumguides.org/docs/scrumguide/v2020/2020-Scrum-Guide-US.pdf The Scrum Guide, November 2020]

| Agile | Scrum |
| --- | --- |
| A set of values and principles for developing software. | A specific framework for organising complex product work. |
| Describes what a team should value. | Defines accountabilities, events, artifacts and commitments. |
| Can be practised with several different methods. | Is one framework a team can use to work in an Agile way. |

Scrum is based on **empiricism**: making decisions from what the team can observe and learn. It uses three pillars:

- **Transparency:** the goal, work, progress and problems are visible and understood.
- **Inspection:** the team frequently checks the product and the way it is working.
- **Adaptation:** the team changes the product or plan when inspection reveals a problem or a better direction.

Inspection without adaptation is only observation. A retrospective that identifies the same problem every week but never changes how the team works is not helping the team improve.

Scrum also names five values that guide the team's behaviour:

- **Commitment:** support each other and work towards the team's goals
- **Focus:** give attention to the Sprint Goal and the work needed to reach it
- **Openness:** be honest about progress, uncertainty, mistakes and blockers
- **Respect:** treat each team member as a capable professional and listen to their contribution
- **Courage:** raise difficult problems, ask for help and change direction when necessary

The events and artifacts only make work visible. These values determine whether the team can discuss what it sees without hiding problems or blaming individuals.

## The Scrum Team

A Scrum Team has three accountabilities:

| Accountability | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Product Owner** | Maximises product value, communicates the Product Goal and orders the Product Backlog. The Product Owner decides priority; they do not assign every developer's task. |
| **Scrum Master** | Helps the team understand and use Scrum, improves the team's effectiveness and helps remove impediments. The Scrum Master is a facilitator and coach, not the team's manager. |
| **Developers** | Everyone who helps create the usable product Increment. Developers plan the Sprint, maintain quality and adapt their plan towards the Sprint Goal. |

In Scrum, "Developer" is an accountability rather than a narrow job title. Front-end, back-end, database, testing, design and delivery work all contribute to the same Increment. Team members may take different tasks, but the product and Sprint Goal belong to the whole team.

A Scrum Team is **cross-functional** and **self-managing**. It contains the skills needed to create value, and its members decide together who does what, when and how. Self-management does not mean working alone without coordination. It means the team owns the plan instead of waiting for one person to distribute every task.

## Work moves through a Sprint

A **Sprint** is a fixed-length cycle in which the team works towards one Sprint Goal and creates a usable Increment. Every other Scrum event happens inside that cycle:

The official Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for the Developers. During the case, the scheduled one-hour check-in can include that short team event as well as tutoring, technical discussion, blocker removal and feedback. The full hour should not become a round of individual status reports.

## Make progress visible

Scrum uses three artifacts, each paired with a commitment:

| Artifact | What it makes visible | Commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product Backlog** | The ordered work that may improve the product. | **Product Goal:** the longer-term result the team is trying to achieve. |
| **Sprint Backlog** | The Sprint Goal, selected backlog items and the Developers' current plan. | **Sprint Goal:** the single objective that gives the Sprint focus. |
| **Increment** | The usable, verified product produced so far. | **Definition of Done:** the shared quality conditions work must satisfy before it counts as complete. |

A ticket is not complete because someone stopped coding. If the team's Definition of Done requires review, tests, integration and deployment to a shared environment, the work remains unfinished until those conditions are met.
